## Data Contracts: Understanding Data Mesh, Standards, and Adoption
### A practical introduction to the anatomy of a data contract

<br>

## Abstract

Teams need data they can trust to make decisions and run their products. Instead, they often get undocumented fields, unclear ownership, and breaking changes that reach production before anyone can act on them.

Data Mesh addresses this problem by putting data ownership with the teams that understand the business context. Data contracts make that ownership usable. They define what the data means, who maintains it, what shape and quality consumers can expect, and how changes are managed.

The talk focuses on the anatomy of a data contract. We will examine its business meaning, ownership, schema, quality expectations, lifecycle, and compatibility rules. We will also look at how standards and tools describe and support these elements in an existing environment.

Attendees will leave with a practical starting point: choose one important dataset, make its promises explicit in a data contract, and give the owning team a simple way to keep those promises. The goal is better data for the people who depend on it, with less coordination overhead.

## Outline

1. **Data Mesh Fundamentals (7 min)**

   * Why Data Mesh: centralized platforms separate data from the teams that understand it
   * Domain-oriented ownership
   * Data ownership stays with the teams that understand the domain
   * Self-serve data infrastructure
   * Federated computational governance

2. **Anatomy of a Data Contract (5 min)**

   * What a data contract describes
   * Business meaning and ownership
   * Schema, quality expectations, and lifecycle
   * Compatibility and evolution

3. **Working with Data Contracts (10 min)**

   * What standards such as ODCS and ODPS bring to data contracts
   * How contract specifications connect to day-to-day tooling
   * How to get business teams to adopt data contracts

4. **How to Start with a Data Contract (3 min)**

   * Choose one dataset and one consumer
   * Write the smallest useful contract around an existing specification
   * Automate one check with the tooling already available
